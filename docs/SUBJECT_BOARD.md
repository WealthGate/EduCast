# Subject board

Choose a subject above the board, then select a tool from the list beside it and click **Use tool**. Click the board to place a diagram or symbol. Use **Select** to move or resize it. Select a diagram and click **Edit…** to toggle its explanatory labels. New pages start with the current subject. **Duplicate page** copies the page so you can work through another example independently.

## World map and globe

In **Geography**, choose **World map** or **World globe**, then **Use tool**. Drag to pan or rotate. Use the wheel or **+** and **−** to zoom. Search for a country, territory, or city to centre it. Use **Labels** and **Grid** to change the view. Choose **Show on Program** to share the atlas with the class.

The offline atlas uses the 1:50 million Natural Earth v5.1.2 vector release. Its generalized borders reflect Natural Earth’s de facto conventions, and dashed lines show its mapped disputes and claims. Some labels follow current UN naming, including **Türkiye** and **Naoero**. Search also accepts their former names. For street detail and a more recently maintained map, choose **Open current area in OpenStreetMap**. This opens an online teaching source. [Natural Earth data and terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-cultural-vectors/), [Natural Earth v5.1.2 files](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/tree/v5.1.2/geojson), [UN member states](https://www.un.org/about-us/member-states), [OpenStreetMap attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

Natural Earth’s map data is public domain. The projection code uses D3 Geo 3.1.1 and D3 Array 3.2.4. [Natural Earth terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/), [D3 Geo license](../apps/desktop/TeachingResources/d3-geo-LICENSE.txt), [D3 Array license](../apps/desktop/TeachingResources/d3-array-LICENSE.txt)

## Interactive instruments

- **Simple pendulum** measures a simulated period and compares it with the small-angle formula. Adjust length, gravity or release angle to start a new run. [Pendulum model](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/15-4-pendulums)
- **Quadrat sampling** lets you place a square in a simulated field and record the number of plants inside it. Take several random samples and compare counts per square metre. [Quadrat method](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/45-1-population-demography)
- **Measuring cylinder** lets you set or randomize a water level on a 100 mL scale and reveal the reading at the bottom of the meniscus. [Graduated cylinder readings](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/1-5-measurement-uncertainty-accuracy-and-precision)
- **Stopwatch** opens the classroom clock in stopwatch mode. Use **Start**, **Pause**, and **Reset** in Studio. Choose **Show clock on Program** when the class should see it.

## Type chemistry

Choose **Chemical equation…** and try `2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)`. EduCast formats formula counts as subscripts and checks atoms and net charge on both sides. Use `<=>` for equilibrium, `^` for charge as in `Fe^3+`, and `e^-` for an electron. Use brackets or parentheses for groups and a middle dot for hydrates, as in `CuSO4·5H2O`. You can place an unbalanced example for the class to solve. **Text…** remains available for prose.

[Chemical notation and balancing](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-atoms-first-2e/pages/7-1-writing-and-balancing-chemical-equations) · [Electron configurations](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-atoms-first-2e/pages/3-4-electronic-structure-of-atoms-electron-configurations) · [Plant and animal cell structures](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells)

## Sketch a shape or symbol

1. Choose **Smart ink**. Draw lines, circles, ellipses, triangles or rectangles in a single stroke. To sketch an arrow, draw from tail to tip, then draw the two wings by returning through the tip.
2. For an equals sign, draw two nearby horizontal strokes in quick succession. In Physics, sketch a resistor as a zigzag or use two nearby vertical strokes for a capacitor. In Chemistry, two parallel strokes suggest a double bond.
3. Pause briefly after the last stroke. Recognized sketches become editable shapes. **Undo** restores the strokes after conversion.

Draw polygons as one closed outline. For a cone, draw the base ellipse and two sides meeting at the apex. The sides may be one stroke or two. For a sphere, draw a circular outline and a flatter ellipse through its center. To place a ready-made cone, sphere, cube, cuboid, cylinder, or polygon, select it from the tool list and drag its bounding area.

## Write mathematical notation

Choose **Smart math ink**. Write one short expression at a time. For a fraction, place a horizontal bar between the numerator and denominator. For a radical, draw a check with an overbar, then write beneath the bar. Add a small 3 at the upper left for a cube root. Write scripts smaller and to the upper or lower right of the base. Draw a dot above and below a short bar for the division sign.

Pause briefly to typeset recognized expressions and equations. EduCast keeps uncertain handwriting as ink. **Undo** restores the original strokes after conversion.

Choose a paper pattern and **Board color…** independently. Use a preset or enter a six-digit hex value. **Auto ink** uses a light or dark color based on the background for new marks. Existing marks keep their selected colors.
